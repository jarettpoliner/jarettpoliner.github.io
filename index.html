Machine learning for physical systems: reconstructing, representing, and simulating complex physical worlds from sparse observations.

I am a postdoctoral research associate at Los Alamos National Laboratory. I develop machine learning methods for physical systems that can only be partly observed: granular and particle-based materials imaged by micro-CT, plastic solids characterized by a handful of experiments, and urban traffic networks with sparse, unevenly placed sensors. My work combines generative models of 3D microstructure, geometric representations that turn point samples into simulation-ready geometry, and learned models of dynamics that forecast when observations are missing. I care about evaluation as much as modeling: comparing generated structures against measured statistics, and reporting where a method falls short as clearly as where it works.

I received my PhD in Civil Engineering and Engineering Mechanics from Columbia University in 2025, advised by Prof. WaiChing Sun.

Selected Research

Recent results from ongoing projects (October 2026): generative modeling and learned geometric representations of granular materials, and traffic forecasting from sparse, multimodal observations. These results have not been peer reviewed; each caption states what has been demonstrated and what has not. Published work is listed under Research and Publications.

Graph-to-mesh generation of granular assemblies

Ongoing · unpublished

A graph model refines a coarse, 12-cluster description of a packing into 100 particles with positions, sizes and shape descriptors, and a shape generator builds a surface mesh for every particle. The measured and generated assemblies below share the coarse description, not individual grains.

Particle geometry as graphs and spherical harmonics

Ongoing · unpublished

Learned models of granular materials need a representation that is compact yet faithful to measured geometry. CT surface meshes are converted to spherical-harmonic shape descriptors and to particle graphs whose edges are inferred contacts; at degree 8 the fitted shapes stay within 2.7% of the grain radius (median surface distance).

Traffic forecasting from sparse, multimodal observations

Manuscript in preparation

With Prof. Xuan (Sharon) Di at Columbia. The forecasting model encodes each observation with its availability and age, and can include or exclude camera and incident inputs at forecast time, so the value of each source can be measured directly. The two study areas sit at opposite ends of observability.

On the public METR-LA and PEMS-BAY benchmarks, whole sensors are withheld at test time. Training with 25% of sensors withheld reduces the extra error at unobserved sensors from +56% to +14% (METR-LA) and from +237% to +51% (PEMS-BAY) when a quarter of sensors remain. In Manhattan, incident reports change forecast error only slightly, and the pre-registered held-out comparison did not confirm an effect.

Research

Learning to reconstruct, represent, and simulate physical systems from sparse observations. In each project below the system is only partly observed: a few CT scans of a granular material, a handful of yield points, or traffic sensors that cover some roads some of the time. I build generative models of what complete states look like, geometric representations (meshes, spherical harmonics, graphs) that keep those states usable by simulation, and forecasting models that stay reliable when observations are missing. Each section says what has been demonstrated and what has not.

01

Generative Modeling of 3D Material Structures

Ongoing

Granular and particle-based materials owe their mechanical response to two coupled structures: how the particles are packed and the shape of each particle. Simulation studies need many realistic 3D microstructures, but micro-CT scans are slow and expensive to obtain. My doctoral work generated such microstructures in two steps: a graph model of the particle-contact network, then a conditional latent diffusion model of each particle's shape. Current work extends this to full 3D assemblies, coupling multiscale graph generation with generation of a surface mesh for every particle. The extension is ongoing, with manuscripts in preparation.

02

Geometric Learning and Reconstruction

Ongoing

Learning on granular materials needs representations that are compact enough to model yet faithful to the measured geometry. I work with three: surface meshes, spherical-harmonic shape descriptors, and particle graphs whose edges encode contacts. On these I am developing graph autoencoders and conditional super-resolution models that recover fine particle assemblies from coarse or incomplete descriptions, for completing partial scans and moving between scales. This work is ongoing, and results will appear here once they are published.

03

Learning Dynamics from Sparse Observations

Manuscript in preparation

City traffic is observed through sparse, irregular sensors: probe vehicles report speeds on some roads some of the time, while cameras and incident reports arrive as separate streams. With Prof. Xuan (Sharon) Di at Columbia, I develop graph-based spatiotemporal forecasting models that track which observations are available and how old they are, and that can include or exclude auxiliary sources at forecast time. The aim is to measure what each source adds beyond observed speeds rather than assume it helps. Experiments cover Manhattan, the Tokyo expressway network, and the public METR-LA and PEMS-BAY benchmarks. A manuscript is in preparation.

04

Physics-Aware Generative Modeling

Published

Plasticity models are usually calibrated from a few expensive experiments, and fitting a closed-form yield function to sparse data takes repeated trial and error. In our CMAME paper, a latent diffusion model trained on a library of yield surfaces infers a dense, plausible surface from measured yield points. The surface is stored as a mesh rather than an equation, and a projection-based stress update replaces the classical return-mapping solve, which makes data-derived plasticity usable in finite-element codes. On the paper's benchmarks, from classical models to discrete-dislocation data for single-crystal copper, the update stays stable and runs in parallel. A companion manuscript on diffusion-based experimental design has been submitted.
